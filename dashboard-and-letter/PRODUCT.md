# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Frontend: React + Vite + TypeScript, plain CSS (tokens as custom properties). Backend: Python 3.11+, FastAPI. Decided by the user; see plan.md.

## Users

- **Margaret (81)**: offline NatWest customer who does not use banking apps. Reads a paper monthly letter. May look at the dashboard with a helper at a hub / Post Office counter, or on her own on a home tablet. Needs large print, plain English, nothing that moves.
- **The helper** (community banker / Post Office / phone team, e.g. Priya): sits beside Margaret, turns a tablet towards her, works the case queue, approves replies.
- **Five more customers, one product** (see `personas.md`): Jayden (18, first account), Amira (recent migrant), Daniel (low literacy), Sam (learning difficulty), Ravi (never budgeted). Same product for everyone; only their generated transaction data differs.
- **Hackathon judges** (NatWest financial inclusion challenge): watch a 3-minute demo and probe the ethics.

## Product Purpose

Common Cents keeps offline customers connected to their money, through paper and people, with AI working behind the scenes. A plain-English monthly letter and a shared dashboard show the same facts. Any question, through any channel, becomes one case; AI prepares and drafts, a human approves. Success: Margaret and her helper look at one clear picture together, and the loop visibly closes (question, answer, confirmation in the next letter and dashboard).

## Positioning

We do not make Margaret go digital. The letter and dashboard are two views of one facts object, rendered from the same data so they can never disagree, and the helper sees exactly what Margaret sees.

## Operating Context

Tablet in landscape at a counter, turned between two people; also a home tablet. Paper letter always arrives. Role switcher (Customer / Helper) and a demo persona picker replace auth for the demo. Demo control page is for judges.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Every figure is computed in code; the LLM only writes words. Number guardrail and banned-content guardrail run on every customer-facing draft.
- Human approval gate before anything reaches the customer.
- Letters contain no links, QR codes or phone numbers other than "the number on the back of your bank card".
- No commercial, credit, upsell or marketing logic anywhere.
- Mock data only, no real customers. British English. Dates in Europe/London.
- Persona bank changed from Lloyds to NatWest (user decision).
- Out of scope: real bank integration, real auth, native app, sending real SMS or post.

## Brand Commitments

- NatWest-style palette (purple and white) on the app screens. The exact brand hex and typeface are unverified and the real NatWest typeface is proprietary, so a free lookalike is used (see plan.md section F1).
- The printed letter carries no logo and no links, so it is hard to spoof (principle 6).
- App footer carries a "prototype, not an official NatWest product" disclaimer (suggested; confirm).

## Evidence on Hand

Nothing real: all data is generated by a seeded generator. No testimonials, customers, benchmarks or NatWest claims may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. Same picture for everyone: letter, dashboard, Margaret and helper never disagree.
2. Code does the maths, the AI only writes, a human approves.
3. Calm and familiar: fixed layout, nothing moves, same order every month.
4. One product for many people: personas differ by data, never by code path.
5. Information and scenarios, never financial advice.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA with AAA-level text contrast where possible. Minimum 18px body (default 20px, large-print mode 24px), semantic HTML, full keyboard operation, visible focus, 48px minimum targets, status never by colour alone, a read-aloud button on every section, reduced-motion respected, no jargon, no dense tables on Margaret-facing screens.
